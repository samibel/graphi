<task>

<what_you_are_given>
A list of 134 questions, one per line, as `<opaque-id><TAB><question text>`, at:

<the_test_you_apply_verbatim>
This is lifted unchanged from the frozen inclusion rule that governs this dataset:

queries, before answerability annotation and before a split is assigned. Two questions are in the
same family when they are exact duplicates, duplicates after Unicode NFC/case-folding/whitespace
collapse/terminal-punctuation removal, paraphrases, or same-task variants. The operational
same-task test is conservative: they must express the same user intent and require the same answer
mode, and one correct answer at the pin could satisfy both without adding a distinct mechanism,
action, or code-defined fact. Merely sharing a topic, identifier, file, or answer span does not make
two different information needs the same task.

Read the last sentence twice. **Sharing a topic, an identifier, a file, or even the same answer
location does NOT make two different information needs the same task.** Two questions about flag
parsing are not one family merely because both concern flags.
</the_test_you_apply_verbatim>

<why_this_matters_and_what_it_is_protecting>
These questions will be split into a development set and a sealed holdout set. If two questions
that are really the same underlying task land on opposite sides of that split, the holdout is
contaminated: the system will have been tuned on a question it is then "blindly" evaluated on.
Grouping them into one family prevents that, because a family never crosses the split.

So the cost of the two errors is asymmetric. **Merging two questions that are not really the same
task** loses a little statistical independence. **Failing to merge two that are** silently breaks
the holdout, and nothing downstream would detect it. When genuinely torn, merge.
</why_this_matters_and_what_it_is_protecting>
